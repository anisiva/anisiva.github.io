Thought leadership

Protecting Children in the Age of AI — Live on Maalaimurasu

On Instagram's end-to-end encryption rollback, 11 child-safety laws across India, the EU, UK, and US, and what every AI/tech leader owes the next generation.

Watch on YouTube ↗
Read the full reflection ↓

This is a long post — but I believe it's one of the most important things I've written. Please read till the end.

This morning, I spoke live on Maalaimurasu about Instagram removing End-to-End Encryption from May 8, 2026.

But the conversation left me thinking much deeper than just one platform's decision.

When I work on AI, I carry a responsibility that goes far beyond the product I build.

Behind every algorithm is a child scrolling at midnight.
Behind every data point is a family that trusts us.
Behind every feature decision is a society that doesn't always get a vote.

We talked about 11 laws across India, EU, UK and USA — all trying to answer one question: how do we protect children in a digital world that moves faster than regulation?

IT Act. DPDP Rules. EU Chat Control. UK Online Safety Act. EARN IT Act.

Governments are trying. Tech companies are navigating. And somewhere in between — a 14-year-old is on Instagram not knowing what "end-to-end encryption" means, or why it matters.

That gap — between what policymakers write and what a child understands — is exactly where leaders like us must show up.

Not just as technologists. But as educators. As parents. As coaches.

Three things I believe every AI/tech leader must do:

  1. Teach the next generation — peers, subordinates, your own kids — what digital privacy means in plain language. Not jargon. Real talk.
  2. Build with intention — every AI feature you ship asks: does this protect or expose the most vulnerable?
  3. Pass it on — the knowledge you carry about data, privacy, AI safety shouldn't stay in boardrooms. It belongs in classrooms, dinner tables, and WhatsApp family groups.

AI is not neutral. It reflects the values of the people who build it.

What values are we passing on?

Honestly, this appearance was less about me sharing expertise — and more a session that made me think. Deeply. About my role, my responsibility, and what I owe to the next generation.
